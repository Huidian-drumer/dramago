# Content Intelligence Human Blind Review Summary

## Hypothesis

A lightweight ContentPlan plus curated Mechanism Retrieval might help CREATE turn identity setup and opening promises into story causality and payoff.

## Method

- Provider: real DeepSeek Flash.
- Three frozen CREATE inputs.
- Same target length, Writer family and Validator.
- Direct Writer compared with the enhanced path.
- X/Y order randomized before human review.
- Human preference recorded before revealing the mapping.

This is a three-case qualitative product experiment, not a statistical benchmark.

## Results

### Case 01 — Hidden heir

Human preference: **Content Intelligence**.

The enhanced draft more clearly converted the hidden identity from setup into causal story pressure and payoff. This is the use case where optional structure added visible value.

### Case 02 — 17-year-old emperor

Human preference: **Direct Writer**.

Additional planning made the protagonist appear too mature and reduced the important constraint that he was seventeen, inexperienced and facing his first court session.

### Case 03 — Regret-vision ability

Human preference: **Direct Writer**.

Direct Writer already developed the central idea that knowing someone will regret a choice does not grant the right to decide for them. Planning did not add enough value to justify its structural weight.

## Decision

**Blind A/B showed selective—not universal—benefit.**

Content Intelligence remains an **Optional Structural Enhancement**, not the default CREATE pipeline.

Recommended when:

- an opening promise is strong but easy to lose;
- identity setup needs narrative consequences and payoff;
- growth needs multiple stages;
- a Direct Writer draft leaves the setting outside the plot.

Not forced when:

- character capability is tightly constrained;
- the story is a single scene;
- the theme is already clear;
- Direct Writer naturally develops the premise.

## Trade-off

In the engineering A/B, the enhanced path had higher total latency because it added a plan stage and a longer Writer context. The experiment therefore supports selective use rather than global enablement.
